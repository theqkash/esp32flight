Everything a window seat wishes it knew

Point it at your Wi-Fi and it starts talking directly to open, free data networks. Your location and history never leave the device.

✈Live radar of your sky

Aircraft within 2–460 km, merged live from three community ADS-B networks (or your own dump1090 antenna). List, detail card, zoomable real-map radar, session stats and a phosphor retro CRT view.

🗺Who, where, whither

Airline with logo, route with cities and flags, progress bar, photo of the exact airframe, ETA in the destination's local time and a spotter line telling you where to look. Routes are position-verified, cross-checked between databases and evicted the moment the flight contradicts them.

🌊More than planes

Optional layers, each one switch: rain radar, airspace zones, weather balloons, harbour ships with destinations, and the ISS when it crosses your sky.

🔔Alerts that find you

Emergency squawks, watchlist hits and „passes over you in ~3 min” pushed to your phone via ntfy.sh; webhooks for Node-RED and n8n; Home Assistant sensors over MQTT.

🖥A real web panel

Every setting in the browser, a live map, spotting history, Prometheus metrics and a documented JSON API with a full data-model reference.

🔧Honest engineering

Hackable C on ESP-IDF with an Android build of the same core for retired tablets. Aviation or metric units, English and Polish, 7 themes, OTA updates with a wrong-board guard, offline map cache, and a wiki that documents every switch.

Not sure about your flash size? Check the metal module: N16R8 = 16 MB, N8R8 = 8 MB, N4R8 = 4 MB. Unbranded 7″ board? See the identification hint under the flash buttons. An old Android tablet (5.0+) works too - same firmware core, packaged as an APK. New boards hatch on the test builds page.

Install in two minutes

Connect the board over USB, click Install, pick the serial port. Everything - firmware, airline logos, maps, airport database - is written in one go. Chrome or Edge on desktop required (Web Serial).

Unbranded 7″ board and not sure which of the two it is? Sellers ship both designs under identical listings. Read the metal ESP32 module on the PCB: N16R8 = the Sunton build, N4R8 = the CrowPanel 7.0 build. A genuine Sunton also has "8048S070" printed on the board. Picked wrong? Nothing breaks - the screen just stays black; flash the other one.

Updating? Leave Erase device unchecked and your Wi-Fi and settings survive. Check it only for a first install.

Android instead? Grab esp32flight-v0.4.5.apk from the latest release. After the first flash, updates go over the air from the device's web panel.

Everything the panel shows is plain JSON on port 80: state, config, backup, Prometheus metrics, live screenshots. Curl examples included.

Experimental firmware for people helping chase specific problems - flashed from the browser, settings survive, going back is one click.

Data it stands on

Community ADS-B (airplanes.live, adsb.lol, adsb.fi) with routes cross-checked against the aircraft's real position; maps by CARTO © OpenStreetMap contributors; rain by RainViewer; weather by Open-Meteo; METAR/TAF from aviationweather.gov; photos by planespotters.net; balloons by SondeHub; ISS by wheretheiss.at; ships by aisstream.io and airspace by openAIP (the last two with your own free keys). The full field-by-field reference lives in DATA-MODEL.md.
